B.1 · Tier B

Burke's Book Store signed promotional poster (Memphis, 2002) #

Item: Promotional poster from Burke's Book Store (Memphis, TN) issued in 2002. Signed by three authors: George Singleton, Donna Tartt, and Corey Mesler (Burke's owner / poet).

Tartt content: None — Tartt's contribution is her signature only. The printed text is Burke's promotional copy.

Cross-reference / collector note: Burke's Book Store, Memphis is documented as a notable Tartt-adjacent venue. See main reference §10.6 (Documented Tartt signing events) and §9.1 (signature characteristics by era — early-2000s sample relevant to authentication). The 2002 date places this signature in the same window as the Bloomsbury UK Deluxe Limited Edition signing programme for The Little Friend (§3.4, October–November 2002). Useful as a co-located signature exemplar.

B.2 · Tier B

TIME magazine, May 5 / May 12, 2014 — TIME 100 Most Influential People (Beyoncé cover) #

Item: TIME magazine double issue, May 5 / May 12, 2014. The annual TIME 100 Most Influential People roster.

Tartt content: Donna Tartt was named to the TIME 100 list in 2014; she does NOT appear on the cover (the visible cover-listed essays and the inside-name list shown on the cover do not include her). Her TIME 100 entry is in the issue's interior. The inside Tartt entry (page range, author of the entry, photograph, and exact text) is not independently confirmed.

Cross-reference / collector note: Tartt's 2014 TIME 100 selection is mentioned in the main reference; this issue is the primary periodical record. Not currently documented in the main reference's §8 magazine inventory. NOTE: some catalogue metadata for this issue incorrectly states "Publication Month: November"; the actual cover masthead reads "MAY 5 / MAY 12, 2014."

B.3 · Tier B

D — La Repubblica delle donne (Italian weekly) — Donna Tartt + Damon Albarn issue #

Item: D — La Repubblica delle donne, Italian weekly women's magazine published as a Saturday supplement to La Repubblica. Cataloguing identifies Tartt and Damon Albarn as the issue's main subjects.

Tartt content: Italian-language interview / feature on Tartt. Issue date and number not visible in available cataloguing and not independently confirmed (the issue date is typically printed on the front cover masthead).

Cross-reference / collector note: Italian-language Tartt press is a thinly-documented vein in the main reference. The other Italian press items in this group (B.5 Il Venerdì March 2014, B.7 Grazia 2014 n.20) all date to the Italian launch period of Il cardellino (Rizzoli, 2014); D — Repubblica may also date to that period, but the date is not independently confirmed.

B.4 · Tier B

Le Figaro Magazine N°21595 — 10 January 2014 #

Item: Le Figaro Magazine (French weekly news supplement), issue N°21595, dated 10 January 2014. Cover headline cluster includes "Le coût des élus" / "Cost of Elected Officials" plus a "D. Tartt" call-out.

Tartt content: French-language feature on Donna Tartt around the French launch of Le Chardonneret (the French translation of The Goldfinch; Plon, 2014). Specific piece type (interview, profile, or review), byline, and page range not independently confirmed.

Cross-reference / collector note: The 10 January 2014 date places this in the French launch window for Le Chardonneret. This is the first French-language Tartt press item in the catalogue.

B.6 · Tier B

Vanity Fair, November 2013 — "Donna Tartt's Glorious New Novel" (Jay-Z cover) #

Item: Vanity Fair, November 2013 (Jay-Z cover photographed by Mario Testino, lead feature: "JAYZ — THE NEW CHAIRMAN OF THE BOARD" by Lisa Robinson). Cover blurb visible at lower edge: "[reading approximate]: DONNA TARTT'S GLORIOUS NEW NOVEL."

Tartt content: A review or feature piece on The Goldfinch (released October 22, 2013 by Little, Brown). The cover-blurb attribution reads as "DAVID HOLLIS" but should be confirmed against the actual contents page; this could plausibly be David Kamp or another regular VF books-section contributor. Page range and exact byline not independently confirmed.

Cross-reference / collector note: Distinct from the more famous Vanity Fair Tartt-related piece — Evgenia Peretz's "It's Tartt — But Is It Art?" (Vanity Fair, July 2014) — which is the post-launch critical-reception controversy article. The November 2013 issue is the launch-window VF coverage and is the earlier of the two VF Goldfinch-related pieces.

Grazia (Italian), 2014 issue 20 — "INTERVISTA ALLA SCRITTRICE PREMIO PULITZER DONNA TARTT" #

Item: Grazia (Italian women's weekly), 2014 issue 20 (cover dated 14/5/2014 = mid-May 2014). Cover model: Elisa Sednaoui. Cover blurb at left side reads: "INTERVISTA ALLA SCRITTRICE / PREMIO PULITZER / DONNA TARTT" (Interview with the Pulitzer Prize-winning writer Donna Tartt). EAN 9771120511004.

Cross-reference / collector note: This is the second confirmed Italian Pulitzer-window Tartt interview in this group (B.5 Il Venerdì March 2014 is the other). The pair documents the Italian press response to Tartt's April 2014 Pulitzer.

B.8 · Tier B

R.B. Russell, Past Lives of Old Books and Other Essays (Tartarus Press, 2020) — contains essay "Fragile Ivory Towers: The Critics and Donna Tartt" #

Item: R.B. Russell's collected essays from Tartarus Press, an English fine-press publisher. Russell is editor of Wormwood magazine and a longtime literary essayist focused on supernatural fiction, decadence, and book collecting.

Tartt content: A standalone critical essay titled "Fragile Ivory Towers: The Critics and Donna Tartt" is one of the 40+ essays in the volume — listed in the publisher's table of contents alongside essays on Arthur Machen, Robert Aickman, Alain-Fournier, Pierre Louÿs, Denton Welch, J.M. Barrie, Quentin Crisp, and others. This is a substantive critical engagement with Tartt's work, not a passing reference. Length, exact subject (e.g., which novel or which critical reception episode), and full text not independently confirmed.

Relevant to a Tartt critical-reception subset (a thinly-documented niche in the main reference). Whether Tartarus Press issued earlier (hardcover or limited) editions of this title is not independently confirmed; the paperback is the current trade form.

B.9 · Tier B

Books to Die For: The World's Greatest Mystery Writers on the World's Greatest Mystery Novels, ed. John Connolly & Declan Burke (Emily Bestler Books / Atria / Simon & Schuster, 2012) — Tana French's essay on Tartt's The Secret History #

Item: A 537-page anthology of 119 essays by mystery writers from 20 countries, each writing about a favorite mystery novel. Hardcover first edition published by Emily Bestler Books / Atria (Simon & Schuster imprint), October 2, 2012, ISBN 9781451696578 (HC) / 9781451696585 (PB/ebook). UK edition: Hachette UK, ISBN 9781444756517.

Tartt content: **Tana French wrote an essay championing Donna Tartt's The Secret History*** as one of the great mystery novels. Per French's quoted essay text: *"For me, this book redefined the territory that mysteries can claim. When I started writing, more than ten years after I first read it, I was writing within a landscape that The Secret History had redrawn for me. I aim to write mysteries that take genre conventions as springboards, not as laws, and never as limitations on quality or scope: books where the real murder mystery isn't whodunit, but whydunit and what it means." An aggregated description further frames French's piece as "the persuasive piece on the importance of Donna Tartt's The Secret History as the first book to use friendship circles as a subject for crime."

Important attribution note: Several dealer-side data feeds (notably an AbeBooks listing, ISBN 9781451696578) include "Donna Tartt" in their contributor-name autolist. This is misleading: Tartt did NOT write an essay for Books to Die For. She is the SUBJECT of Tana French's essay (which discusses TSH). The dealer-side conflation of "subjects of essays" with "contributing authors" is a common data-aggregator error and should not be treated as evidence of a Tartt-authored contribution. A signed-by-26-contributors copy of the first edition exists; Tartt is NOT among the 26 signers.

References:

  • Simon & Schuster product page: simonandschuster.com/books/Books-to-Die-For/John-Connol…
  • Goodreads: goodreads.com/en/book/show/15743453-books-to-die-for
  • Hachette UK edition: hachette.co.uk/titles/john-connolly-2/books-to-die-for/…

Cross-reference / collector note: Tana French wrote In the Woods (2007) and the Dublin Murder Squad series; she is part of the post-TSH wave of literary-crime writers who explicitly cite Tartt as foundational. French's essay is the highest-profile critical engagement with TSH from another working novelist in the published record. Useful adjacent material to the main reference's §3181 "Critical reception" area and the §16 "Awards and critical reception" section, and to a Tartt-related criticism-by-other-novelists subset.

The volume is also notable for the breadth of mystery-genre coverage: 100+ essays, signed copies (one listing carries 26 contributor signatures), with contributors including Michael Connelly, Patricia Cornwell, Kathy Reichs, Ian Rankin, James Lee Burke, Sara Paretsky.

Book Magazine, November/December 2002 — Liz Seymour cover-story interview on Tartt #

Item: Book Magazine (West Egg Communications, NY), November/December 2002 issue, softcover. Contains a substantial cover-story interview with Tartt by Liz Seymour titled "The Adventure of the Vanishing Lady-Writer." This issue is the source of the Liz Seymour piece already documented in the main reference's primary-source interview index at P04 / O04 (reference/interviews/originals/2002-11-12_BookMagazine_Liz-Seymour_Adventure-of-the-Vanishing-Lady-Writer.md).

Tartt content: Cover-story interview around the launch of The Little Friend. Only the first ~600 words of the Liz Seymour piece have been recovered (per O04 notes in the interview index — Book Magazine folded in 2003 and the full text was lost from the LIV reprint). The print issue is therefore the only documented complete source of the interview.

Cross-reference / collector note: CONFIRMED DUPLICATE — this is the issue containing the already-documented O04 Liz Seymour interview. A physical copy is the only complete source for an otherwise-unavailable primary-source Tartt interview (the full text was lost when Book Magazine ceased publication); it would complete the truncated O04 transcript.

B.11 · Tier B

The Modern Library: The 200 Best Novels in English Since 1950 by Carmen Callil and Colm Tóibín (Picador, London, 1999) — Tartt's The Secret History is in the canonical list; one documented multi-author signed copy bears Tartt's signature at her own entry #

Item: A reference work / canon-list by Carmen Callil (founder of Virago Press) and Colm Tóibín (Booker-shortlisted novelist) selecting 200 best English-language novels published 1950–1999. First UK edition, Picador, London, 1999. Hardcover with dust jacket. Donna Tartt's The Secret History (1992) is one of the 200 listed novels.

Tartt content: Two layers of significance:

  1. As subject (Tier B base): Callil & Tóibín's entry for The Secret History in the 200-list constitutes one of the earliest book-form canonical placements of Tartt's debut novel — only seven years after publication (1992 → 1999). The entry text by Callil/Tóibín is critical-evaluation prose about TSH and would be of interest to anyone tracking critical reception of TSH.
  2. As signer of a documented unique copy (Tier-A overlap): A specific signed copy held by Ashton Rare Books (Leicestershire, UK) is signed by both Callil and Tóibín plus 57 of the contributing/listed authors — each signing at the page of their own entry. Donna Tartt is among those 57 author-signers, having signed at her TSH entry. Other signatories on the same copy include Martin Amis, Margaret Atwood, Ian McEwan, Bret Easton Ellis, Salman Rushdie, Kazuo Ishiguro, plus several Nobel laureates. This makes the Ashton copy a multi-signed Tartt-bearing artifact.

Bibliographic record:

Reference:

  • Ashton Rare Books, Leicestershire, UK — listed on Biblio.com (biblio.com/details.php?dcx=1551305587) for the multi-signed copy. Per the dealer description, the copy carries 59 total signatures (the two authors + 57 contributors signing at their respective entries).

Cross-reference / collector note: This is a documented Tartt-signed reference-work copy in the same family as the Lenox Hill chapbook program (§5.2 / §8.3 / §10.7) and the Folio Society 500-copy signed limited (§7.14 / CON-011a). Should be cross-referenced when §10.9 (signed contributed-piece editions) is next revised — though the framing differs: in §10.9 Tartt is the contributing author, here she is the subject of someone else's entry but added her signature to that entry on a specific copy.

This is also one of the earliest documented Tartt signatures on a non-Tartt-authored book (signed-at-her-entry signing programs are an emerging Tartt-collecting category, distinct from inscribed-personal-copy and dedicated-to-someone signing).
