5.1 "Sleepytown: A Southern Gothic Childhood, with Codeine"#

Cover: assets/images/magazines/HARPERS-1992-07-cover.gif — MAG-001 (Harper's July 1992 - Sleepytown)

  • Venue: Harper's Magazine, vol. 285, no. 1706, July 1992, pp. 60–66
  • Harper's TOC classification: [Article]; subtitle: "A Southern gothic childhood, with codeine." URL: harpers.org/archive/1992/07/sleepytown
  • Full text is reproduced at languageisavirus.com — languageisavirus.com/donna-tartt/short-fiction-sleepyto… — a third-party fan site whose text reflects the original publication. Genre: short memoir/fiction.

Fig. 5.1 — Harper's Magazine, vol. 285, July 1992 — contains Tartt's "Sleepytown: A Southern Gothic Childhood, with Codeine," pp. 60–66.

  • Genre note: Although catalogued in this section under "Short Fiction" for historical continuity (and despite its reprinting in a fiction anthology), "Sleepytown" is an autobiographical memoir — Tartt's account of her own Southern childhood. The Harper's magazine header identifies it as a personal essay/memoir. Its placement in §5 reflects its original magazine context alongside other short-form Tartt pieces.
  • Republished (and renamed) in Cowboys, Indians and Commuters: The Penguin Book of New American Voices, ed. Jay McInerney (Penguin, 1994)

Canonical URL: harpers.org/archive/1992/07/sleepytown (Harper's digital archive; subscriber-only PDF) Text status: Harper's digital subscription required for the original; full text reproduced at languageisavirus.com. 5.2 "Tam-O'-Shanter"#

Cover: assets/images/magazines/NEWYORKER-1993-04-cover.jpg — MAG-002 (The New Yorker April 1993 - Tam-O'-Shanter)

  • First publication: The New Yorker, April 19, 1993, pp. 90–95
  • The New Yorker article URL: newyorker.com/magazine/1993/04/19/tam-o-shanter (accessible to subscribers). TNY's TOC labels it [Fiction]. Cover of the April 19, 1993 issue: "Biting the Bullet" by George Booth. The story concerns Gordon, an elderly former British child actor (once "Geordie MacTavish the Highland Lad"), visiting a nine-year-old leukemia patient in a children's hospital in San Diego. Opening sentence: "The Children's Hospital was cheery enough, thought Gordon—as far as hospitals went." Tagged: Actors, Old Age. Full text is reproduced at languageisavirus.com — languageisavirus.com/donna-tartt/short-fiction-tam-o-sh… — a third-party fan site whose text reflects the original publication.

Lenox Hill Bookstore signed limited chapbook (2004): issued in 2004 as part of a 5-chapbook series letterpress-printed by Stinehour Press in Vermont for Lenox Hill Bookstore in New York. Each title in a numbered limitation of 250 copies signed by the author (200 for sale; 50 hors-commerce). Companions in the same series:

  • Colum McCann — Hunger Strike
  • Jennifer Egan — You (Plural)
  • Lynne Tillman — The Substitute
  • Aleksandar Hemon — The Drawer

Canonical URL: newyorker.com/magazine/1993/04/19/tam-o-shanter (The New Yorker, Apr 19, 1993; paywalled). Lenox Hill chapbook (2004): print only. Text status: TNY subscription required for the full story; full text reproduced at languageisavirus.com. Cover image: assets/images/rId60.jpg (Fig. 5.2 — Lenox Hill chapbook 2004; Vandello Books/Biblio photo)

Foreign-language appearance: German translation as "Schottenmütze" in Sommer, Sonne, Sand und mehr: Das große Ferien-Lesebuch III, ed. Georg Reuchlein (Goldmann Verlag, Munich, 1995; ISBN 9783442429455), pp. 96–101. See §5.7 for full bibliographic detail.

Other contributors to the 10-story anthology: Robert Olen Butler, Richard Ford, Fred Chappell, Tim Gautreaux, Lee Smith, Julia Ridley Smith, plus preface by Barry Hannah and watercolor paintings by Wyatt Waters. 5.5 "The Ambush"#

Cover: [no standalone cover — see source publication] — MAG-005 (Guardian Weekend June 25 2005 - The Ambush)

Venue: The Guardian Weekend (Saturday magazine supplement), Saturday, June 25, 2005. Pages 17–19 and 21 of the Weekend supplement (Guardian Weekend, format 246×292mm). Page 20 not confirmed (likely a full-page illustration or advertisement between pp. 19 and 21).

  • Guardian_2005-06-25_TheAmbush_p17.pdf
  • Guardian_2005-06-25_TheAmbush_p18.pdf
  • Guardian_2005-06-25_TheAmbush_p19.pdf
  • Guardian_2005-06-25_TheAmbush_p21.pdf

Full bibliographic detail: The Guardian (London, Greater London, England), Saturday, June 25, 2005. Section: Guardian Weekend supplement. Title: "THE AMBUSH / BY DONNA TARTT" (as printed). Story runs pp. 17–21 of the Weekend magazine.

Content: First-person short story. Narrator: Evie. Opening line: "Before I met Tim — who, in spite of everything I'm about to tell you, would be my best friend for the next four or five years — my mother warned me on the way over to his grandmother's house that I had to be nice to him." Tim Kendrick, age 7 (narrator Evie is 8); Tim's father was killed in Vietnam. The story is set in a suburban neighbourhood and centres on the children's elaborate war games (playing Vietnam), Tim's mother Mrs. Cameron, Mrs. Cameron's absent daughter-in-law Rose, and culminates with Mrs. Cameron falling down the porch stairs in a domestic accident during one of the children's game episodes, ending with Evie running from the scene in panic. Genre: contemporary literary short fiction.

Reprinted in: The Best American Short Stories 2006, edited by Ann Patchett (guest editor), Katrina Kenison (series editor). Publisher: Houghton Mifflin, October 2006. ISBN: 978-0-618-54538-8 (hardcover). This is the most accessible anthology source.

Collector significance: The Guardian Weekend, June 25, 2005 is an original periodical appearance (the story also appeared in Tin House, Vol. 7 No. 2, 2006, and was reprinted in The Best American Short Stories 2006); four pages of the Weekend supplement (pp. 17–19, 21). Guardian Weekend back issues are scarce. The Best American Short Stories 2006 (Houghton Mifflin, 2006) is the best anthology source and more readily available.

A piece titled "Canis Major" is sometimes attributed to Tartt as a short story in The Oxford American, but no primary or secondary evidence supports this; the Oxford American's own author page lists no such title, and the attribution should be disregarded.

Canonical URL: Print only — Guardian Weekend, June 25, 2005 (pp. 17–19, 21); primary scans in assets/articles/. Anthology: BASS 2006 (ISBN 978-0-618-54538-8). Text status: Print only — no Guardian online archive confirmed; primary newspaper scans held locally. Cover image: None documented — Guardian Weekend supplement; no cover image archived

5.6 "Het land van de Papaver" ("The Land of the Poppy")#

Cover: assets/images/TLF-NL-BEZIGE04-cover_ebay.jpg — MAG-006 (Het land van de Papaver - De Bezige Bij 2004)

Tartt contributed a 42-page work titled "Het land van de Papaver" (Dutch: "The Land of the Poppy") to a 20-volume boxed set published by De Bezige Bij (Amsterdam) in 2004 to celebrate the publisher's 60th anniversary.

Bibliographic block:

  • Title: Het land van de Papaver (Tartt is volume 13 of 20 in the boxed set)
  • Author: Donna Tartt
  • Publisher: De Bezige Bij, Amsterdam
  • Date: 2004
  • Format: small hardcover (gekartonneerde band)
  • Pagination: 42 pp
  • Trim size: 10.5 cm × 16 cm
  • Series: De Bezige Bij 60-jarig jubileum (60th-anniversary commemorative series, 20 volumes total)
  • Companions in the set: Jan Cremer "Verloren gedichten"; J.A. Deelder "JA Beelder"; Anne Tyler "Jouw plaats is leeg"; Gerard van Westerloo "De zaak M"; Nicolaas Matsier "Roma amoR"; Imre Kertész "De verbannen taal"; John Irving "Pension Grillparzer"; Harry Mulisch "Anekdoten rondom de dood"; H.J.A. Hofland "Boven en onder de grond op Schiermonnikoog"; Willem Frederik Hermans "Het behouden huis"; Allard Schroder "Terugkeer"; Graham Swift "Het hart van onze Nicky"; Tartt "Het land van de Papaver" (vol 13); Remco Campert "Ohi, hoho, bang, bang"; E.L. Doctorow "Een huis op de prairie"; Daniel Mason "De man die kleur maakte"; Gyorgy Konràd "De schrijver en de stad"; Gerrit Komrij "Kraaivanger"; Leon de Winter "De Vijand"; Jessica Durlacher "Nieuwbouw."

Significance: "Het land van de Papaver" is the Dutch translation of "Sleepytown: A Southern Gothic Childhood, with Codeine" — the autobiographical Harper's Magazine essay documented in §5.1 (first pub. Harper's Magazine, 1992, 7 pp). The 42-page length in the De Bezige Bij set reflects expanded typesetting at the small 10.5×16 cm format. Source for the English-title identification: BoekMeter.nl.

Provenance: De Bezige Bij has had a long publishing relationship with Tartt — it published the chronological-true-first Dutch firsts of TLF (De kleine vriend, Sept 15, 2002, before US Knopf) and TG (Het puttertje, Oct 2, 2013, before US/UK Little Brown). The 60th-anniversary set in 2004 falls between these two De Bezige Bij Tartt titles.

Auction record: the complete 20-volume set sold at Catawiki on Feb 4, 2016 (NL) for USD $5,090 [29]. Individual volumes of the Tartt 42-page hardcover also circulate separately (Format: Hardcover; Publisher: De Bezige Bij; Year: 2004; ISBN-10: 9023416066 / ISBN-13: 9789023416067; translator: Babet Mossel; Language: Dutch). Babet Mossel also co-translated TLF (De kleine vriend, 2002) for De Bezige Bij.

Authentication notes:

  • The 20-volume set is the canonical issuance — individual-volume copies of "Het land van de Papaver" should be regarded with caution unless they include the De Bezige Bij 60th-anniversary publishing context (publisher's slipcase or set documentation)
  • Set should include all 20 volumes; missing volumes substantially reduce value
  • Dimensions 10.5 × 16 cm small hardcover (gekartonneerde band, 90 pp average per volume) are diagnostic
